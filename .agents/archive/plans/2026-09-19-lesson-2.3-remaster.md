### The corrections that were measurements, not opinions

**Q2 — the `import-control.xml` reorder.** The learner's half A claimed the DTD forces the root `<file>` block to come after all `<subpackage>` blocks. Both halves were re-tested today by reordering the real file and running `mvn -B checkstyle:check`, restoring it between runs:

- Hoisting the root `<file>` blocks above the root `<allow>` lines **fails to parse**: `The content of element type "import-control" must match "((allow|disallow)*,(subpackage|file)*)"`. So the DTD forbids `file` before `allow`, and says nothing about `file` before `subpackage` — those share a group and interleave freely. Half A is wrong as stated.
- Hoisting the root `<file name=".*Test">` above the `<subpackage>` blocks is **legal XML and produces 19 violations** across `TerminalGriddlerTest`, `CLIParserTest` and `GriddlerEngineTest`, which lose `java.nio` and `model`. Half B is the real answer, and the dangerous reorder is the one the DTD lets through.

**Q6 — the glyph deferral.** The answer assumed Chapter 3 and beyond leave the terminal for JavaFX or a Spring API. `lessons/SYLLABUS.md` has Chapter 3 as "Seeing the Board", a terminal renderer, and the browser not until Chapter 10. The premise is contradicted by the chapter's own syllabus.

**Part 1's `x`.** The box claimed `Main` prints a grid of `#`, `.` and `x`. Neither generator emits `UNKNOWN` — `grep` over `GriddlerEngine` and `Main` returns nothing, and a default run prints only `#` and `.`. `Cell.UNKNOWN` is reachable from test fixtures and from no execution path at all.

### The finding worth keeping

**Appendix A has no "unknown" row.** Its four glyphs are Filled `█`/`#`, Empty `·`/`.`, Marked `✕`/`X`, Deduced-this-pass `▓`/`+`. `Cell.UNKNOWN` carries `"x"` — Marked's ASCII fallback, lowercased. The constant is named for one concept and glyphed as another, and the two are genuinely different cells: *marked* is a player's assertion that a square is empty, *unknown* is the absence of one. That is the second type close-out Q8 was circling, and it answers the question the learner asked back. It is also why the answer is "more than three states on a player board", not "more than two".

**A lesson whose boxes are all ticked stops being read.** Four of the corrections here were visible from the code the whole time; none was found until the lesson was read line by line against the tree. The `Must be true` box added to Part 3 — "no box anywhere in this file is ticked against a statement the tree contradicts" — exists so the next reading is cheaper than this one.
